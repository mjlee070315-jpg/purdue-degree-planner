Check off courses as you complete them. The scheduler re-solves the remaining prerequisite graph on every change: it prioritizes courses on the longest remaining dependency chain (the critical path to the capstone), then packs each semester up to your credit cap. This is a greedy heuristic for what is really an integer program — decision variable x[course, semester] ∈ {0,1}, prerequisite + credit-cap constraints, objective: minimize semesters to graduation.
18
How the scheduling model works
Nodes: each major's required/elective courses, sourced from Purdue's published plans of study and department handbooks.
Edges: prerequisite relationships between courses.
Constraint: a course can only be scheduled once every prerequisite is scheduled in a strictly earlier semester, and a semester's total credits ≤ your cap.
Objective: minimize total semesters, tie-broken toward keeping the officially recommended sequencing.
Optimality check: every time the schedule updates, the tool also computes a combinatorial lower bound — the larger of (a) total remaining credits ÷ credit cap, rounded up, and (b) the longest remaining prerequisite chain. No valid schedule can beat this bound, so when the greedy result matches it, the schedule is provably optimal — no ILP solver required. When it doesn't match, the gap is shown; closing it exactly is NP-hard at this scale (this is a precedence-constrained scheduling problem, related to P|prec|Cmax), but the greedy result is guaranteed to be within that gap of the true optimum.
Caveat: this mirrors each department's published curriculum but isn't the authoritative source — always confirm against myPurduePlan / DegreeWorks and an academic advisor before registering.
